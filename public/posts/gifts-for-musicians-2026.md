## Thoughtful Gift Ideas for Musicians

Finding a good gift for a musician doesn't have to mean spending a fortune. Whether they play guitar, bass, violin or keyboards, useful gear and playful musical gadgets can make practice more enjoyable without stretching the budget. Here are ten ideas for musicians at different stages, from essential maintenance tools to a pocket-sized synthesiser.

Prices are approx and may change.

### 1. Positive Grid Spark GO (£85)

The Spark GO is a tiny, portable guitar amp that packs in 33 amp models and 43 effects. Its companion app adds a large ToneCloud library, song chord detection and Smart Jam backing parts, while Bluetooth lets it double as a speaker. It works with guitar and bass, and the rechargeable battery is rated for up to eight hours of play.

It is a handy gift for a player who wants a grab-and-go practice setup, or something small enough to take on a trip. The amp can also be used with headphones for quieter sessions.

### 2. Ernie Ball Tool Kit (£33)

Guitarists and Bassists who like to take care of their own instruments will get plenty of use from this compact maintenance kit. It includes a string cutter, polishing cloth, 6-in-1 screwdriver, ruler, string winder, WonderWipes and a set of inch hex wrenches.

It brings several small but useful tools together in one place, making string changes and basic adjustments easier. It is intended for guitar maintenance rather than specialist repair work.

<a href="https://www.thomann.co.uk/ernie_ball_tool_kit_451436.htm" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">https://www.thomann.co.uk/ernie_ball_tool_kit_451436.htm</a>

### 3. Mechanical Metronome (£22.79)

A traditional pendulum metronome gives musicians a steady visual and audible pulse while they practise. This wind-up model has selectable beat accents and a classic wood-grain finish, so it can sit on a piano or music stand ready to use without opening an app.

It is a simple way to work on timing and consistency, particularly for beginners. There are plenty of these available on Amazon, in different styles and finishes.

<a href="https://www.amazon.co.uk/Metronome-Guitar-Violin-Mechanical-AUPHY/dp/B08JYMBVP1" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">https://www.amazon.co.uk/Metronome-Guitar-Violin-Mechanical-AUPHY/dp/B08JYMBVP1</a>

### 4. Soundbrenner Pulse (£99)

The Soundbrenner Pulse takes a different approach to keeping time: instead of clicking in the room, it vibrates against the player's body. It can be worn on the wrist, arm or leg, and its LEDs flash in time with the beat.

The Pulse works on its own or with the companion app, supports tap tempo and custom time signatures, and can sync with up to five devices. MIDI and DAW support make it especially useful for musicians who practise or perform to a set tempo without wanting an audible click.

<a href="https://www.thomann.co.uk/soundbrenner_pulse.htm" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">https://www.thomann.co.uk/soundbrenner_pulse.htm</a>

### 5. Behringer CS400 Compressor/Sustainer (£15.70)

For guitar and bass players, the CS400 is an inexpensive way to experiment with compression. It can even out volume differences between louder and quieter notes and add sustain, with dedicated Level, Tone, Attack and Sustain controls for shaping the effect.

It is an appealing first compressor pedal for someone curious about dynamics and sustain. The 9V power supply is not included, so check whether they already have a compatible pedal supply or battery.

<a href="https://www.amazon.co.uk/Behringer-CS400-Compressor-Sustainer-Effects/dp/B002X8Y4SQ" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">https://www.amazon.co.uk/Behringer-CS400-Compressor-Sustainer-Effects/dp/B002X8Y4SQ</a>

### 6. Stylophone Mini WH (£20.90)

The Stylophone Mini is a pocket-sized analogue synthesiser with a built-in speaker and a metal-foil keyboard played using the included stylus. This small remake of the classic 1970s instrument adds vibrato and an LED, and comes with its two CR2032 batteries.

It is more of a playful musical toy than a full keyboard, which makes it a fun stocking filler for a curious musician or anyone who enjoys unusual sounds.

<a href="https://www.thomann.co.uk/stylophone_mini_wh.htm" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">https://www.thomann.co.uk/stylophone_mini_wh.htm</a>

### 7. Yamaha CS40 II 3/4 Classical Guitar (£92)

The Yamaha CS40 II is a good-quality, 3/4-size classical guitar suited to younger players and anyone who finds a full-size instrument difficult to manage. It has a spruce top, meranti back and sides, a nato neck and a 580 mm scale length.

Its smaller body and shorter scale make it a comfortable starting point for lessons, while its established build quality makes it a more considered choice than the cheapest beginner instruments. This is the guitar only, so a case or gig bag may need to be bought separately.

<a href="https://www.thomann.co.uk/yamaha_cs_40.htm" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">https://www.thomann.co.uk/yamaha_cs_40.htm</a>

### 8. Audio-Technica ATH-M50x Headphones (£127)

The ATH-M50x is a dependable all-rounder for listening, home recording and monitoring. Its closed-back, over-ear design helps isolate outside sound, and the large drivers are designed to deliver clear, detailed audio. The cable is detachable, with multiple cable options included.

Studio headphones are a useful gift for musicians who record or practise with backing tracks. As with any headphones, comfort and sound preferences are personal, but the M50x is a familiar, versatile choice.

<a href="https://www.amazon.co.uk/Audio-Technica-ATH-M50X-Monitor-Professional-Headphones/dp/B00HVLUR86" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">https://www.amazon.co.uk/Audio-Technica-ATH-M50X-Monitor-Professional-Headphones/dp/B00HVLUR86</a>

### 9. Harley Benton HBV 990BEM 4/4 Electric Violin (£125)

This full-size electric violin is an affordable way for a violinist to explore amplified playing or practise more quietly through headphones. It has a maple body and neck, a headphone output, a microphone/MP3 input and a line output for an amplifier.

The set includes a case, shoulder rest, composite bow, rosin and a 9V battery. An electric violin is quieter acoustically than a traditional violin, though it will still make some sound when played unplugged.

<a href="https://www.thomann.co.uk/harley_benton_hbv_990bem_4_4_electric_violin.htm" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">https://www.thomann.co.uk/harley_benton_hbv_990bem_4_4_electric_violin.htm</a>

### 10. Casio CT-S200 WH Set (£138)

This white Casio bundle is a strong starter option for someone learning keys. The CT-S200 has 61 keys, 400 sounds, 77 rhythms and built-in learning features, plus USB MIDI and a connection to Casio's Chordana Play learning app.

The set includes a keyboard stand, headphones, power adapter and music holder, so it is ready to set up at home. At just 3.3 kg, the keyboard is also easy to move or put away after practice.

<a href="https://www.thomann.co.uk/casio_ct_s200_wh_set.htm" rel="noreferrer" target="_blank">https://www.thomann.co.uk/casio_ct_s200_wh_set.htm</a>

## Conclusion

From a pocket-sized amp to a complete keyboard bundle, these gifts cover a lot of musical ground without getting into the cost of a new full-size instrument. A useful tool or practice aid can be just as appreciated as a fun gadget, especially when it suits the way the recipient likes to play.
